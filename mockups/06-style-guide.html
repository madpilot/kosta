A garden grows
in the system, too.

The design language for Sprout — editorial, warm, quietly confident. Forest as the surface, cream as the page, tomato as the punctuation. Italic Fraunces does the talking; Inter does the UI. Hand-drawn touches keep it from feeling sterile.

1. Color

Forest is the page; cream is paper. Tomato accents only on action, warning, or emphasis — never decorative. Water/Soil/Plant/Prune are categorical: each task type owns one tint.

2. Typography

Two families. Fraunces for everything that wants voice — display, italic, marginalia. Inter for everything functional — buttons, body, data, labels. The hand-drawn character lives in Fraunces' SOFT axis, not in a third typeface.

Default to SOFT 100 for italic display and marginalia (rounded terminals, hand-set feel). Drop to SOFT 60–80 for upright section labels and panel headings where formality reads better. Avoid SOFT 0 — it loses the warmth of the system.

5. Buttons

One primary tomato button per surface. Use ghost-light on green backgrounds, ghost-dark on cream. Forest-fill for secondary actions inside dark surfaces (like the user's chat bubble).

6. Pills & badges

Pills carry one job: type. Always 11.5px Inter 600, dot on left, category color. Never decorate.

Water Harvest Fertilize Plant / sow Prune Healthy

The ✦ glyph appended to a pill indicates the task was generated by Sprout. Manual entries get no glyph.

8. Agent & chat

The agent — Sprout — has an avatar (cream tile + sprout glyph), a name, and a wobbly bubble. The user gets a forest-fill bubble with the corner cut on the right. Don't mix the corner directions.

10. Hand-drawn elements

The system has personality, not whimsy. Use these sparingly — at most one per surface. They're seasoning, not the dish.

Scribble underline

the garden

Use: directly beneath display headings, never on body type. Always tomato-500. SVG path with a 3px round-cap stroke.

Marginalia — italic side-notes

↳ harvest before they split!

Use: small editorial asides next to dense info. Italic Fraunces, SOFT 100, ~14.5px, rotate -2 to -4°. Tomato pill background when on cream; bare text when on forest. Don't use for critical information — they're commentary.

Highlight & scribble

The peach highlighter calls out a fact within prose. The tomato scribble underline calls out an action verb or a verdict.

Wobbly bubble outline

I'm a Sprout message. My border traces a slightly imperfect path — like someone outlined me with a pencil.

Use: agent's first / featured message in a thread. Don't apply to every bubble — the wobble loses meaning at scale.

Sketch arrows

like this — pointing at things

Bezier curve, 2.2px stroke, round caps, tomato-300 (or tomato-700 on cream). Always pairs with an italic Fraunces note.

12. Illustration

Plant illustrations are flat, no shading, with a tight palette: forest stems, two greens for leaves, tomato for fruit. Stems use 2px round strokes that stop just short of meeting — keeps them feeling drawn, not engineered. Avoid gradients except on highlights and pots.

13. Voice

Sprout — the agent — is your knowledgeable friend who happens to know everything about your plot. Not a chatbot. Not a teacher. The voice is specific, observed, and confident — never apologetic, never enthusiastic about its own capabilities.

specific observed quietly confident warm without being saccharine brief grounded in your garden

Do / don't

Specific over generic

Reference exact data Sprout has — the soil test, the suburb, the day count. "Probably not nitrogen — at day 64 on a Sungold, lower-leaf yellowing is usually just shedding."

Generic plant advice

Never sound like a wikipedia article. Never start with "It depends!" "Tomatoes can yellow for many reasons. Common causes include nitrogen deficiency, overwatering, and disease."

Direct verdicts

The user wants an answer. Lead with it. "Yes — prune them off cleanly with a sharp snip."

Hedge-stacking

Don't bury the answer in qualifications. "Pruning can be a good option in some situations, although there are reasons you might want to wait…"

Plain language, real measures

Litres, centimetres, mornings, "before sundown". "2L every two days, deeper when it's hot."

Marketing-speak or jargon

Avoid "leverage", "robust", "powered by AI", "unlock". Avoid Latin unless it earns its place. "Leverage AI-powered insights to optimize your photosynthetic outcomes."

Show the source

When citing a fact, footnote it briefly. "¹ Based on your soil test (12 Mar): N-medium, P-low, K-high."

Bare assertions

Don't claim the soil is low in nitrogen if Sprout's only inferring. Cite, or say "I'd guess".

Words we use

Bed, plot, cordon, truss, sucker, side-dress, plant out, sow, tidy, flush (of fruit), blush (turning ripe), split, last frost, zone. Australian/UK gardener vernacular — friendly, specific.

Avoid: plants will love this!, amazing harvest awaits, let's grow!, green thumb, exclamation points outside marginalia.
